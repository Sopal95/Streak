  • 6 August 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 13 minutes

    Pacita Abad, the quilted atlas of a world

    Pacita Abad invents trapunto: she paints then stuffs and sews the canvas, incorporating mirrors, buttons, shells, and textiles collected from sixty countries. She installs her works, paints a bridge in Singapore, and places the migrant experience and mask rituals at the center of a maximalist and committed practice.

  • 5 August 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 11 minutes

    Ambreen Butt: the art of counting

    Ambreen Butt combines Indo-Persian miniature painting and contemporary collage to explore identity, gender, and political violence. Her works, woven from tea-stained paper, resin, and torn text, give names and faces back to lives that history too often reduces to mere anonymous statistics.

  • 4 August 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 11 minutes

    Joan Snyder, the grid, the body, and the overflow

    Joan Snyder paints abstract canvases where she glues straw, petals, thread, and papier-mâché onto grids drawn in pencil. A former sociology student, she combines geometric rigor and raw material to tell, without detour, the experience of women and the body.

  • 3 August 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 10 minutes

    Mary Cassatt: The Invisible Work of Women

    Mary Cassatt paints domestic gestures and women's work with an almost geometric precision, borrowed from Japanese prints. An American painter and printmaker living in Paris, the only American admitted among the independents, she imposes a clinical observation where sentimentality was expected.

  • 2 August 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 10 minutes

    Jean Cocteau, continuous line and vertigo

    Jean Cocteau traces, on walls, stained glass, ceramics, and paper, a unique and continuous line, borrowed from gesture more than from academic modeling. He doubles this graphic economy with a geometric requirement: mirrors, apse symmetries, calculated glazes. Considered a minor draftsman by some, he imposes a singular method where movement and measure balance without contradicting each other.

  • 1 August 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 14 minutes

    Käthe Kollwitz: engraving pain

    Käthe Kollwitz engraves and sculpts the grieving social body: weavers, mothers, widows, insurgents. Resistant to rising abstraction, she refines a stripped-down figurative language where the gesture, raised hand, bent back, embrace, alone carries the dramatic charge. Her work articulates private sorrow and collective catastrophe, without yielding to pathos.

  • 31 July 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 13 minutes

    The fingers of Louis Soutter in the night

    Louis Soutter, Swiss draftsman and violinist, produced nearly three thousand drawings between 1923 and 1942 in a hospice in the Vaud Jura. Trained in Brussels, Paris, and the United States, he built a visual syntax where musical rigor and the violence of history come together with absolute coherence.

  • 30 July 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 10 minutes

    Marcel Moore, the other hand

    Marcel Moore, Suzanne Malherbe by her real name, is the visual architect of one of the most radical bodies of work of French surrealism. Illustrator, photographer, photomontage designer, she practiced for fifty years an art of indirect action: her images do not impose meaning, they establish it through gaps and resonance.

  • 29 July 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 12 minutes

    Claude Cahun: under this mask, another mask

    Claude Cahun has been composing photographic self-portraits since the 1910s where masks, costumes, and double exposures unsettle all certainty about gender and identity. Neither reducible to surrealism nor any other movement, her work, created with Marcel Moore, remains a solitary investigation of the self as perpetual fiction.

  • 28 July 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 10 minutes

    Yaacov Agam or art as a living event

    Yaacov Agam based his work on a radical idea: painting cannot remain static. His polymorphic reliefs present different images depending on the angle of view, making the spectator an essential actor. By moving, the visitor creates the composition themselves, each position revealing a new visual reality.

  • 27 July 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 10 minutes

    Judy Chicago or the politics of the triangle

    Judy Chicago articulates a radically collective and historiographic artistic practice: she creates monumental installations that restore to women a place in cultural memory from which they had been systematically erased. Her work operates at the junction of feminist claim, documentary rigor, and technical virtuosity.

  • 26 July 2026 ❖ Art Critique ❖ 10 minutes

    Delita Martin and the art of the veil

    Delita Martin, a Texan artist combining engraving, painting, and hand sewing, patiently reconstructs the visual presence of Black women erased from art history. Heir to the tradition of Elizabeth Catlett, she develops a composite visual language blending African aesthetics and Western techniques to impose sovereign and absolute portraits.
